# What is comparable across the archives?

**Snapshot retrieved 2026-11-20 UTC.** The unit of a transit fit is a KOI. The unit of an independent stellar comparison is a KIC. Use one row per KIC for population density tests. Both planets in each of the nine fitted pairs inherit the same stellar inference. The project directory contains the inputs needed to repeat the joins offline.

## 2017 benchmark

The three author-supplied summary tables yield 44 occultation KOIs, 13 tidal KOIs and 18 planets in nine compact systems. Verify exact KOI suffix and KIC before joining. The excluded, unsuccessful fits in systems 156, 723, 1805 and 1824 are outside this sample. The GitHub tree at f0e9b34c8a744a609c3eb49e4239e58088cd51cc and the v1.0.1 release referenced by Zenodo have identical file trees. There is a sample file for every KOI. File existence alone establishes neither convergence nor independent host posteriors. [Repository](https://github.com/esandford/KnowThePlanet) · [Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1028515) · [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07293)

The journal catalog independently identifies the same 75 KOI–KIC pairs. All five central values agree. Nine uncertainty fields differ at published rounding precision across the 1,125 median/error comparisons. The reconstruction preserves the authors' repository values. The differences are listed in journal_check.json. [Journal data](https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/viz-bin/VizieR?-source=J/AJ/154/228/catalog)

In original_sample.csv, t0 is BKJD, P is days, b and radius ratio are dimensionless, and log10rhostar is base-ten log of density in kg/m³. Each E_ is the positive distance to p84 and each e_ is the positive distance from p16 to the median. Exponentiate each density quantile separately. The three physical-density columns reflect the rounded table precision. The eclipse priors are Gaussian in e cos ω and e sin ω. The published posterior columns instead contain square-root-e components. Alpha columns are the authors' sampling coordinates for nonlinear limb darkening. Section 2.3 states LC plus SC where available. We distinguish inference from present-day cadence availability from the direct Fig. 3 identification. Warnings combine named paper cautions with a grazing screen using marginal medians. A blank warning establishes no claim about all samples in a chain.

**Physical precision changes the benchmark.** Counting each host once and defining fractional half-width as (ρ84 − ρ16)/(2ρ50), the rounded author tables give a median of 19.0% across 66 stars. Only 3 of 66 reach 5% by that definition. For KOI-929.01 the released draws give −12.5%/+15.4% about the physical median. The paper's quoted 62% reaching 5% should not be used as a physical-density performance target without reconciling the definition and scale. The three qualifying hosts are those of 1541.01, 1075.01 and the 1779 pair. Each carries a paper warning, and the first two now have false-positive dispositions. The CSV includes the transformed endpoints and fractional half-width so the calculation can be repeated.

## Reconstructed 929.01 photometry

The 15 raw FITS files cover Q2–3 and Q5–17. Header checks establish KIC 9141746, LC mode, DR25, the two flux columns in electrons/s, and TDB barycentric times with BJD reference 2454833. Phase is time from the nearest transit in days. We use the cumulative linear ephemeris (174.633553 BKJD, 6.491682808 days, duration 4.301 h). Across 63,266 input rows, 7,790 have a nonzero SAP_QUALITY. Largest gaps between accepted-quality samples within a quarter range from 0.94 to 11.38 days. Companion masking removes 2,947 additional good cadences across the quarter files, including out-of-transit times. Kepler-730 c uses 132.1455 BKJD, 2.85188338 days and 2.666 h. The mask pads its half duration by one cadence. We apply no extra dilution correction to SAP. Quarterly CROWDSAP spans 0.9947–0.9982.

Following Section 2.3 we form windows with half duration h = 1.1 T14/2 + one LC and baseline extension √10 h. We require three baseline points on both sides, a two-cadence span, nearby baseline within two cadences of the mask, and at least three in-transit points. We discard invalid or flagged samples. Our outlier rejection uses a preliminary polynomial baseline, a robust residual scale and a 4σ cut outside transit or upward in transit. The paper used a 21-point moving median, 3σ thresholds and its own coverage cuts. We fit a degree 0–3 polynomial by weighted least squares and out-of-transit BIC **once**, then divide SAP and its error by the fitted trend. The paper selected degree outside transit and re-fitted the trend during each likelihood evaluation. Our intervals condition on the fitted baseline and omit trend uncertainty.

Of 198 predicted centers within the observed quarter spans, 152 satisfy these cuts, versus 189 catalog transits and 183 retained by the paper. The three counts have different definitions. An exact replication would need the authors' accepted-cadence mask. We preserve 6,650 unbinned points and a separate 60-bin folded input spanning ±0.18 days. SAP uses inverse-variance weights; formal errors assume independent cadences. PDCSAP uses the same selected times and SAP weighting with its own baseline and is shown as a sensitivity check. The training labels are prior draws from the simulator. The two figures, all individual event decisions and quarterly bit counts are under results/data_quality.
